Health and Safety

Please read this before playing, and make sure children who play have it explained to them.

Photosensitive Seizures

A very small number of people may have a seizure when exposed to certain light patterns or flashing lights, including those that appear in video games. This can happen even to people with no history of seizures or epilepsy. If you, or anyone in your family, has ever had a seizure or epilepsy, consult a doctor before playing.

Stop playing immediately and consult a doctor if you or your child feel dizziness, altered vision, eye or muscle twitching, loss of awareness, disorientation, any involuntary movement or convulsions. Parents should watch children while they play.

Your Play Space

You play by moving your hands and arms in front of the camera.

  • Clear the space around you. Keep away from people, pets, furniture, walls, windows and anything that could break or hurt you.
  • Stand or sit about 1 to 2 metres from your device, with room to stretch both arms out in every direction.
  • Put the device on a stable surface or stand where it cannot fall. Do not hold it while you play.
  • Watch where you are moving. Do not play near stairs or on a slippery floor.

Take Breaks

  • Rest for 10 to 15 minutes every hour, even if you don't feel you need it.
  • Stop if your hands, wrists, arms, shoulders, neck or eyes feel tired, sore, numb or painful, and see a doctor if the feeling does not go away.
  • Do not play when you are very tired or unwell.

Children

Children should play with an adult nearby who has read this information.

Your Device

The camera and games make your device work hard, so it can get warm during long sessions. If it gets hot, take a break and let it cool. Avoid playing in direct sunlight or while it is charging if it is already warm.
